# Kettlebells (issue #112) reconstruction notes

Sources checked 2026-09-19. Photos were downloaded and reviewed as contact sheets (8–25 per product). Popularity is from Gym Radar (`/most-owned/kettlebells`, `/equipment/<slug>` pages). Gym Radar's "Rogue Kettlebells V1" and "Rogue Kettlebells" are the same imported colour-striped Rogue line (V1 is the original powder-coat run, now ductile iron), and its "Kettlebell E-Coat" / "Kettlebell Powder Coat" are the two finishes of the Made-in-USA Rogue bell. They ship as two entries with params.

## Shared geometry

- Z up, flat base on the floor, handle loop in the XZ plane (X along the grip), weight side facing −Y. The handle centreline is a cubic horn leg (root sunk into the body, straight rise to the widest point) plus a superellipse top (`square` = exponent; 2 round, 4 boxy), swept as a hull of spheres with a short fillet flare at the root (`bellLayout` in `floor-parts/kettlebells.ts`). Footprints, heights and the builder all read that one layout.
- Classic cast body: a sphere cut flat at the published base diameter. Solid-iron density under-sizes the body (cast bells are cored), so body diameters come from product photos: Rogue's own 4/16/40/92 kg photos give body ÷ handle width = 0.68 / 0.81 / 1.17 / 1.30, height ÷ handle width = 1.19 / 1.26 / 1.54 / 1.70 and a horn root angle of 53° / 45° / 32° / 30° from the top (`ROGUE_PHOTO_RATIOS`). Brands that publish only weights use that body curve (`castBodyDiameter`) with the Yes4All published height / width / grip chart.
- Competition body: two half-ellipsoids meeting at the widest circle (34% of the height), crown between the horns at 67%, flat base 0.68 D, measured on the Kettlebell Kings and Titan photos. Standard dimensions: 280 mm height, 210 mm body, 35 mm handle (IUKL/IKO/GSU via kettlebell.university).
- Markings: weight numerals in a plain 4 × 6 stroke font (digits and KG/LB only), debossed 1.5 mm into the sphere, raised or printed on a flat machined panel. Brand wordmarks are blank debossed / raised plates; no logo artwork.

## Review tooling

Besides `scripts/shoot-part.ts`, a local Python z-buffer renderer (mesh dump + orthographic front/back/iso clay renders) was used for fast render-vs-photo rounds while the shared machine was saturated; the committed evidence images come from the app viewer.
